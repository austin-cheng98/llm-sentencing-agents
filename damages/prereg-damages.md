# Preregistration: civil-damages transfer of the attribution premium

Registered before any decision in run D1 was collected. The manifest and the code
that builds every prompt are hashed in `damages/FREEZE-damages.txt`.

## Question

In the sentencing experiment, agents pulled harder toward displayed numbers when
those numbers were attributed to other judges than when the identical numbers were
attributed to a statistical forecast. This run asks whether that premium is a
property of the sentencing task or of how agents read attribution. The task changes
from imposing a custodial sentence to entering a monetary settlement valuation on a
civil bodily-injury claim. The estimator does not change.

## Why this domain

Damages valuation shares the three features the estimator needs: a bounded numeric
judgment, a defensible range derived from the file, and a setting where showing an
agent other parties' figures is realistic rather than contrived. Both framings
exist in practice, since claims units circulate adjuster valuations and insurers
deploy valuation models. The domain is also distinct from sentencing in the way the
reviewer's concern requires: civil not criminal, monetary not custodial, and the
decision-maker is an adjuster rather than a judge.

One feature of the domain cuts the other way and is handled by design. Comparable
awards are arguably legitimate evidence in a valuation, whereas peer sentences are
not supposed to anchor a sentencing judge. A pull toward peer figures could
therefore be defended as appropriate updating on informative evidence. Both primary
arms consequently carry a word-for-word identical sentence stating that the
displayed figures were produced from the same file and from no other information.
In the sentencing study this equivalence cue was a follow-up; here it is in the
confirmatory arms.

## Claims

Sixteen fictional claim files, a full 2^4 factorial over four binary factors:
injury severity, liability clarity, claimant mitigation, and documentation
completeness. The advisory midpoint is a stipulated function of the factors and the
advisory range is the midpoint plus or minus 12.5 percent, matching the sentencing
range width. The policy limit is $1,000,000 in every claim. Claims are generated by
`scripts/gen_claims.py` and stored in `cases/claims.json`.

## Arms

The advisory range is withheld in every arm. Agents are told that Westmoor practice
sets no advisory range and that they may enter any amount from $0 to the policy
limit. No pull measured here can be attributed to an institutional anchor.

Primary, structure-matched and carrying the equivalence cue:

- `dpeer_ng` header, one descriptive sentence, the equivalence cue, three rows
  attributing the figures to other adjusters of this unit.
- `dtool_ng` header, one descriptive sentence, the equivalence cue, three rows
  attributing the identical figures to a regression model fitted to historical
  settlements.

Secondary, header and rows only, with no descriptive sentence and no equivalence cue:

- `dpeerbare_ng`, `dtoolbare_ng`.

The displayed figures are byte-identical across the arms of a pair. The prompts
differ only in the header, the descriptive sentence, and the row labels.

## Displacement

Three figures are displayed whose mean sits exactly at the midpoint displaced by
delta, drawn from {-0.30, -0.15, +0.15, +0.30}, at 0.94, 1.00 and 1.06 of the
displaced midpoint and rounded to the nearest hundred dollars. Displacement is
assigned by partitioning the sixteen design points into four groups that are each
balanced on all four factors and rotating the group-to-displacement mapping across
agents. The code asserts that every displacement level is exactly balanced on every
factor before any prompt is built.

## Primary sample

Twelve fresh agent contexts, sixteen claims, two primary arms: 384 decisions
forming 192 matched agent-claim pairs. Every cell is attempted once. A cell that
fails to parse is recorded with `ok` false and is not re-drawn, and the pair it
belongs to drops out of the matched analysis.

## Outcome and estimator

The outcome is proportional deviation from the advisory midpoint,
`dev = (award - mid) / mid`. Pull is the OLS slope of `dev` on `delta` within an
arm. The premium is the difference in slopes, estimated as the coefficient on the
`delta x arm` interaction. Standard errors cluster on claim. The p-value comes from
randomization inference with B = 9,999 and seed 17, permuting the displacement
allocation within agent.

The minimum detectable effect at 80 percent power and a two-sided 5 percent test is
2.8015852185999996 times the clustered standard error.

## Benchmark and decision rules

The benchmark is 0.206, the structure-matched premium estimated on Claude Opus 5 in
the sentencing task.

- The transfer replicates if the premium is positive, the interval excludes zero,
  and the MDE is at or below the benchmark.
- The transfer fails if the interval excludes zero in the negative direction, or if
  the interval excludes the benchmark while containing zero.
- The run is inconclusive if the MDE exceeds the benchmark, whatever the point
  estimate. An interval that contains both zero and the benchmark is reported as
  inconclusive and not as a null.

The secondary pair is interpreted descriptively and is not used to decide whether
the transfer replicated.

## Model and collection

Claude Opus 5, one fresh subagent context per decision, no tools available. The
prompt is passed verbatim with nothing added before or after it. The harness
re-derives each prompt at record time and stores its SHA-256, so a prompt that
drifted from the frozen manifest is detectable after the fact.

## What this run cannot settle

It compares one model lineage on one additional domain. It provides no human
baseline, so it cannot say whether the premium is a human tendency the model
inherited or an artifact of how models read attribution. It does not vary the
realism of the valuation model's described provenance, so a reader who holds that
adjuster figures remain more informative than model figures even under the
equivalence cue is not answered by this design.
